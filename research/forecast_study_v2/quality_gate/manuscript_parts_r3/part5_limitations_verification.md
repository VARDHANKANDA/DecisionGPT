## 11. Limitations and threats to validity

* **Internal freezes, not public pre-registration.** The v2 and v3 protocols were hashed before the relevant outcomes were read, but the hashes are not externally timestamped; the v2 design was set with knowledge of the v1 results and an internal audit of them, and the v3 design with knowledge of the v2 results.
* **Window exposure.** For v2, W2 and W3 targets of the evaluated entities were not scored before; their calendar *periods* were used as training data and, for other series, in a retired benchmark, and a price-identification regression used the targets in-sample (documented). W1 for CA_1, TX_1 and Online Retail II is non-independent. Online Retail II has no W3 and a short W2 history (487 days). The v3 window W4 and stores CA_2, TX_2, WI_2 were never scored for any entity before, but are public M5 data from the same retailer and the design is not blind to earlier findings.
* **Baselines and models.** 31 prespecified simple baselines in v2, plus ETS, ARIMA and Theta in v3 only (fitted once per block with information handicaps, restricted ARIMA search; Section 5). Eight learned configurations with shared hyper-parameters and no tuning; no neural, probabilistic or hierarchical models; CatBoost not evaluated. "Beats the best simple baseline" must not be read as "beats the best forecasting method".
* **Selection.** One model per endpoint is chosen from eight candidates on three validation blocks; validation margins are small (Section 8.3). In the W2 exploratory refit the selected model was the best of eight in seven of eight cells, but test scoring of non-selected candidates was not part of the frozen protocol and cannot be used to revise verdicts.
* **Sampling.** 350/349 department-stratified series per M5 store (v2: series launched after d_1100 excluded; v3: after d_848) and the 100 largest-revenue Online Retail II SKUs; results describe these samples. The eligibility rule (days with a listed price) is an assumption about what a forecaster knows.
* **Dependence and inference.** Intervals are percentile or t-based and mildly liberal in simulation on synthetic structures (worst-case coverage ≈ 0.90–0.95 at nominal 0.95); the aggregate endpoint has 24 weekly blocks; the iid-week bootstrap ignores weekly autocorrelation (visible in TX_1 H28); the item-day methods share the independence-of-weeks assumption (Section 8.2); model errors across blocks are correlated through overlapping training windows (not modelled); one seed per primary model. Holm adjustment is within hypothesis families, not across them; only H1 is primary.
* **Metric choice and utility.** WAPE and its aggregate version are the prespecified endpoints; WAPE is a wMAPE-type measure that Kolassa [2016] considers unsuitable for count data, and it rewards forecasts near the conditional median on sparse series. The item-day winner is strongly downward-biased; the gain does not extend to asymmetric loss (Section 8.4); no cost, service-level, probabilistic or proper-score evaluation exists, so **no operational or financial benefit is shown**.
* **Dual-method rule.** It converts method disagreement into *inconclusive*, which is conservative for H3; its item-day methods are not independent in their dependence assumptions.
* **Same-agent development and verification.** The study code, the second code path, the supplementary and quality-gate analyses and this manuscript were produced by one AI assistant under the author's direction. No external or independent validation has taken place (Section 12).
* **Data rights.** M5 data and any array containing or reproducing M5 sales are not released.

## 12. Reproducibility and verification

**Tests.** The v2 study tests (17) and the v3 tests pass in the project environment; the v2 tests also pass in a fresh environment with identical pinned versions. The repository suite (`pytest tests`) gives the counts reported in `quality_gate/FINAL_VERIFICATION_REPORT_R3.md`.

**Second code path.** `verify_independent.py` (written by the same assistant) imports nothing from the study and recomputed targets from the raw files, all 31 baselines row by row, metrics, improvements, bootstrap and cluster-robust intervals (B = 4,000), Holm adjustments, verdicts, the validation-only selection and the ordering of selection before test fits: 98/98 checks passed in W2, 72/72 in W3 and 98/98 in W1; point estimates were identical in 88 of 88 comparisons and 87 of 88 verdicts agreed (the exception, W2 TX_1 H28 H3, is resolved in Section 8.5). This path **takes the stored model forecasts as input**; it does not re-fit the models. In the quality-gate audit the headline improvements were additionally recomputed from the stored predictions with separate code (maximum difference 2e-16 over 22 cells).

**Clean-environment reproduction of all 22 cells.** Every (window, dataset) pair of the v2 study was re-executed from the raw data with the study's pipeline in a fresh Python 3.12.0 environment with the pinned package versions, writing into a scratch directory outside the repository (about 2 h 55 min on the 4-core machine, under load). In all 22 cells the selections, configuration hashes, validation tables, {{NUM:e1_methods}} method summaries and {{NUM:e1_arrays}} stored forecast and baseline arrays were identical (maximum absolute difference 0.0), and the verdicts, improvements and p-values recomputed by the frozen analysis code from the re-run were identical (Table R3-4). **Limits:** same machine, operating system and processor count; the raw data were not re-downloaded; the pinned versions were identical, so sensitivity to library versions was not tested; the code is the same, so this shows execution reproducibility, not correctness.
