Every table is generated from stored result artifacts by `tools/build_tables.py` and `quality_gate/tools/build_tables_r3.py` (source files are named under each table); figures are generated by `report.py` and `quality_gate/tools/make_figures_r3.py`. Improvements are relative reductions in WAPE pooled over the six test blocks, (E_ref − E_model)/E_ref. Intervals are 95 % percentile intervals from the crossed bootstrap (item-day) or the iid-week bootstrap (subset-day); "Holm p" is adjusted within the eight cells of the window.

### 7.2 Model selection on validation data

For the item-day endpoint the absolute-error LightGBM was selected in all eight W2 cells (31-leaf configuration on M5, the 15-leaf / minimum-child-100 configuration on Online Retail II). For the subset-day endpoint the Tweedie (p = 1.1) model was selected in six W2 cells and the squared-error model in the two WI_1 cells; the absolute-error and quantile models were never selected for the aggregate endpoint in any window. In W1 the 0.5-quantile model replaced the absolute-error model in two cells (CA_1 H28, TX_1 H7). The best validation baseline for item-day was a trailing or weekday median (trailing 14–91 days; weekday median of 8 weeks on Online Retail II); for subset-day it was a weekday mean (4 or 8 weeks). No cell selected the all-zero forecast. **The validation margin between the selected item-day model and its runner-up was small (Section 8.3).**

## 8. Sensitivity analyses and replication

*Labels: Sections 8.1 (seeds) and 8.5 (H3 precision check) are supplementary; 8.2–8.4 are post-hoc and exploratory analyses added by the quality gate on stored or re-fitted v2 results. None replaces or changes a frozen result.*

### 8.1 Seed sensitivity (W2, descriptive)

Refitting the item-day model with seeds 43 and 44 changes the H1 improvement by at most 0.4 percentage points (per cell 0.09–0.40 pp; Table A2); the lowest of the three seeds is ≥ 2.3 % in every cell. Seed variation is not included in the intervals.

### 8.2 Weekly dependence of the item-day comparison

The two item-day interval methods both treat the 24 weeks as independent clusters of the same cell-total array. The weekly differences between baseline and model errors are positively autocorrelated in many cells (lag-1 autocorrelation up to {{NUM:max_lag1}}; AR(1) effective sample as low as about 5 of 24 weeks in WI_1 H28 and Online Retail II H7). Recomputing H1 and H2 with weeks resampled in stationary blocks (mean three weeks) and applying the frozen decision rule leaves **all {{NUM:h1_same_all}} H1 verdicts and all {{NUM:h2_same_all}} H2 verdicts of the three windows unchanged**; the interval widths grow by at most {{NUM:max_widen_pp}} percentage points (Table R3-1). The dual-method rule therefore corroborated the item-day verdicts, but its two item-day methods share the dependence assumption, so the sensitivity analysis — not the rule — is the evidence for robustness to weekly dependence.

### 8.3 Selection margins and selection robustness

Validation margins between the selected model and its runner-up were small: 0.11–0.83 % of validation WAPE for the item-day endpoint in W2 (typically against the 0.5-quantile objective, a near-twin of absolute error) and 0.02–1.08 % across all windows, smaller than the margins between the best and second-best baseline in several cells (Table R3-2). To test whether the result belongs to the particular selected configuration or to its objective class, **all eight candidates were refit on the six W2 test blocks** (post-hoc, exploratory; the selected models' results reproduced the frozen ones to 2e-16). The validation-selected model had the best test item-day WAPE of the eight in seven of eight cells (second, by 0.01 percentage points, in CA_1 H7). The three LightGBM median-type candidates (absolute error, absolute error with smaller trees, 0.5 quantile) beat the best matched baseline in all eight cells (+1.7 % … +6.0 %), XGBoost with absolute error in seven of eight (−1.7 % … +3.9 %), and all four mean-type candidates (Tweedie 1.1 and 1.5, squared error, ridge) were worse than the best baseline in every cell (−34.0 % … −1.5 %). The item-day result thus belongs to the median-type LightGBM class rather than to a lucky configuration, and no winner's curse is visible *in this comparison*; XGBoost shows that "median-type objective" alone does not guarantee a gain.

### 8.4 Asymmetric loss (exploratory, illustrative)

No cost data exist. As an illustration of whether the item-day gain depends on the symmetric absolute-error criterion, the improvement of the item-day-selected model over the best matched baseline was recomputed under pinball loss at τ = 0.5 (absolute error), 0.7, 0.8 and 0.9, which penalise under-forecasting more (Table R3-3, Figure 4; crossed bootstrap B = 2,000, no multiplicity control). The improvement shrinks as τ grows; at τ = 0.9 the point estimates are negative in {{NUM:pin_neg_09}} of 8 cells (the interval excludes zero on the negative side in TX_1 H28 only) and the interval excludes zero on the positive side only in WI_1 H7. The H = 7 cells of TX_1, WI_1 and Online Retail II retain positive point estimates at every τ; CA_1 H7 turns negative from τ = 0.8. **The item-day advantage is therefore established only for (near-)symmetric absolute error, not for service-level-oriented loss, and a WAPE improvement does not show decision value.**

The second code path (B = 4,000) classified this cell as supported whereas the frozen analysis (B = 10,000, fixed seed) classified it as inconclusive because the two prespecified methods disagreed. **This analysis is supplementary: added after the results were known, with the same two prespecified methods and the unchanged decision rule; it does not replace the frozen result.**

With 3 × 10^6 draws per method (three new seeds) Monte-Carlo error is negligible (seed SD ≤ 0.0002). The primary method's Holm p is 0.0514 and the secondary method's 0.0433: the methods still disagree and the frozen rule gives *inconclusive*. The frozen verdict is confirmed. All 22 H3 verdicts of the three windows are unchanged under the converged calculation (`SUPPLEMENTARY_H3_RESOLUTION.md`). The weekly differences in this cell are positively autocorrelated (lag-1 +0.36; AR(1) effective sample ≈ 11 of 24 weeks); the cell is best described as suggestive, not established.

### 8.6 Exploratory results

Gains of the item-day model are concentrated in frequently selling and high-volume series (Table A3, W2; for example CA_1 H28: frequent series +8.1 % [+3.5, +12.7], n = 46; intermittent or sparse +2.5 % [+0.5, +4.7], n = 304; low and mid volume terciles with intervals that include zero). These segment intervals (B = 2,000) are not corrected for multiplicity and are descriptive. The design effect of the crossed series × week structure on the standard error is 1.5–2.5 (Table A4).
