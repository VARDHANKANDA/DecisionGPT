# VENUE_READINESS

Evidence-based review of the three proposed venues, 2026-10-22. Information below was read from each venue's **own pages** on that date unless marked otherwise. Nothing was submitted, no account was created, no editor was contacted, no fee was paid. Acceptance likelihood is **not** inferred from prestige or from the fact that a result is negative.

## 1. What the current contribution is (the standard against which fit is judged)

After this audit the defensible contribution is **an empirical, descriptive case study with a negative primary result**: on two M5 stores and one retail wholesaler, a validation-selected mean-targeting gradient-boosting forecaster does not beat the best simple baseline on item-day pooled WAPE, and the ranking reverses when demand is summed over days or items; the reversal is mostly the baseline's level bias. The theoretical reason is established (the optimal point forecast depends on the loss; absolute error is minimised by a median - Gneiting 2011; Kolassa 2020; Prestwich et al. 2014), so the paper is **not** a methodological discovery. It becomes a stronger methodological contribution only if the revision adds a metric-matched confirmatory arm (see AUDIT_SUMMARY, next steps). The manuscript also contains an inactivity-classification study whose evidence is weaker and whose label is not churn.

## 3. Likely objections by a reviewer at each venue

**Common:** (1) the central observation is an instance of known results on loss functions and point forecasts (Gneiting 2011; Kolassa 2020) and on error measures for intermittent demand (Prestwich et al. 2014; Hyndman & Koehler 2006) that the manuscript largely does not cite; (2) the primary comparison pits mean-targeting models against a median baseline under a median-optimal metric, so the negative primary result is largely predetermined; the matched-objective model appears only as a post-hoc exploratory experiment; (3) three datasets, 350-series subsets, one seed, small grid, no statistical/ETS-type baseline family beyond naive/moving/Croston, no deep or foundation models; (4) aggregate metric defined on a subset, not the M5 hierarchy or WRMSSE; (5) the "pre-registration" is internal; (6) interval coverage is carried by zero demand.
* **IJF:** novelty and contribution to forecasting practice ("what is new relative to Gneiting/Kolassa/Prestwich and to the M5 paper's own multi-level WRMSSE design?"); the data-posting requirement conflicts with the M5 terms; comparability with M5 (the setting is not the competition's).
* **TMLR:** weaker ML-methodology content; claims must be fully supported - the abstract's generalisations (Section 17-21) would be narrowed; reviewers will ask for stronger baselines (global statistical models, probabilistic or foundation models) and seeds; heightened scrutiny because the study and the draft were produced with an AI assistant (the AI-use policy above).
* **PLOS ONE:** data availability - M5 data cannot be redistributed (non-commercial, no redistribution) so the data statement must document the restriction and the access route; abstract length; reporting of limitations; sufficiency of the replication rationale.

## 5. Recommendation (by fit to the actual contribution and standards)

* **Best fit for the contribution as it stands after a corrective revision: TMLR.** Its stated criteria (support claims with accurate evidence; lack of novelty is not a reason for rejection), no author fee, double-blind review, unrestricted length and arXiv-compatible posting match an honest, narrowed, negative-result evaluation study. The required human responsibility for AI-assisted work must be met genuinely by the authors.
* **Best topical fit: IJF** - its scope names evaluation and forecasting practice. It is the better recommendation **only if** the revision closes the novelty gap (cites the loss-function and intermittent-demand-measure literature, states what is new, and adds the metric-matched confirmatory arm and the aggregation-level analysis in the M5-hierarchy spirit). Cost if the open-access route is chosen: USD 3,500 excl. taxes (price personalised at submission; a subscription route exists). The IJF data-posting rule requires written clearance for M5-derived data.
* **PLOS ONE** is acceptable for a sound negative result but is the least aligned with the forecasting audience, its fee is **unverified**, and its public-data rule collides with the M5 terms; use only if the authors resolve the data-rights question and confirm the fee.
* None of the three should be chosen before the blockers are resolved; none was contacted.
