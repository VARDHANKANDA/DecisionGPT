# Literature review and research-gap analysis

**Status: bounded, NOT a systematic review.** Scope: forecast-evaluation methodology, retail/intermittent-demand forecasting with machine learning, uncertainty
(conformal) and calibration metrics, as needed to position `EXPERIMENT_PLAN.md`. It was produced in one session with web access.

**How verification was done.** Bibliographic fields (title, authors, journal, volume, issue, pages, year, DOI) of every journal article marked V1 were read from the
publisher-deposited Crossref record of the DOI. Items marked V2 were confirmed from the proceedings or arXiv page; where a page range could not be confirmed it is
omitted rather than guessed. "Findings" below are limited to what the abstract (or the cited page) states; where only bibliographic data was verified the finding
column says so. One DOI I recalled from memory for Syntetos & Boylan (2005) was wrong and was corrected by this check; a recalled CatBoost page range was off by one
and was corrected. Nothing below was taken from memory without a check.

## 2. Synthesis

1. **Established:** global gradient-boosted trees are strong on M5-like retail demand (L4, L15, L12); rolling-origin evaluation, scaled errors and careful metric
   choice are established best practice (L5, L6, L7, L8, L17); intermittent-demand estimators (Croston/SBA/TSB) are the standard simple baselines for sparse series
   (L9-L11); conformal prediction is a standard distribution-free interval method with known exchangeability caveats (L22, L23).
2. **Contested or under-reported:** how much of the learned-model advantage survives when the reference is the *strongest simple baseline* rather than seasonal naive,
   and how large it is relative to the sampling uncertainty of one forecast origin. I did not find a source that reports this together with a crossed series x time
   dependence-aware interval for point-forecast WAPE on public retail data; **this is an absence in what I read, not proof of absence.**
3. **Overlap that limits novelty:** L12 (ML vs statistical, daily SKU), L5 (evaluation pitfalls), L31 (local vs global on intermittent demand) already cover the broad
   territory.

## 3. Research gap (stated narrowly, and conditionally)

Within the sources read, the specific gap is **protocol-level empirical evidence**: for public retail datasets, (a) the ML-vs-baseline gap measured against the best
validation-selected simple baseline, (b) over multiple chronological origins, (c) with an uncertainty estimate that respects both cross-sectional and temporal dependence,
(d) split by demand-frequency segment, and (e) reported at both item and store aggregate level. This is an incremental empirical contribution. It is **not** a new
method, and its significance depends on the results, which may show no advantage.
